# Rider Level Calibration

## Level criteria

| Level | Criteria (training history first, W/kg as support) | Coaching focus |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner | Regular riding under 3 months, or <15 rides in the last 90 days / under 2 h per week; W/kg typically <2.2 | Build the habit, aerobic base, body and traffic adaptation |
| Developing (regular amateur) | At least 3 months of riding, 2–4 sessions per week; W/kg roughly 2.2–3.5 | Structured work begins (sweet spot / threshold first) |
| Trained | Systematic training, ≥4 sessions per week sustained; W/kg >3.5, or has completed structured cycles | Follow the defaults of the other skills |

Priority of evidence: user identity/goal text (phrases like "just started riding") > training history and frequency (dossier: rides in 90 days, weekly hours; look further back via monthly summaries) > W/kg. W/kg is strongly affected by sex and age — treat it as support only; if the athlete parameters are still factory defaults or clearly inconsistent with the riding data (e.g. an IF that cannot be squared with the configured FTP), the W/kg criterion is void — assign the level from training history alone. When two levels are plausible, choose the lower one. State the inferred level in reports/advice ("at your current riding volume I would place you at the beginner stage") so the user can correct it.

## Level-specific conventions (this section overrides the generic defaults in other skills)

- **Beginner**: the central task is riding more and more regularly, not harder. 2–4 sessions per week (at least 1 rest day), at most 1 intensity session per week starting with short sweet-spot blocks (e.g. 2×10 min; the standard sweet-spot template is about 50 min, and when time is short the generator falls back to a short version that fits around 30 min including warm-up and cool-down); no VO2max, anaerobic or sprint prescriptions; weekly duration growth ≤10–15%; "one extra ride / 20 more minutes" outranks "more intensity". Do not push beginners into 20-minute all-out FTP tests (build 2–3 months of data first; automatic estimation and parameter-update tools stay available — this rule only bars sending the user out for an all-out test; keep estimates conservative). Plans spanning 4 calendar weeks or more with sessions in each week still include one recovery week (see "Weekly Planning Rhythm (Scheduling by Day of Week)").
- **Developing**: structured training may begin (sweet spot/threshold first, light VO2max), 1–2 quality sessions per week with ≥48 h spacing.
- **Trained**: follow the default conventions.

## Expectation calibration (beginner: these are normal, not problems)

- Low cadence (60–80 rpm), HR drift above 10%, slow Z2 speed, high VI on rolling terrain, low TSS/CTL, no interval or peak-power history — never conclude "failing / regressing / weak base" from these; state the normal range for a beginner first, then discuss improvements.
- Trained-rider yardsticks (VI<1.05, drift<5%, polarized distribution, etc.) are not beginner pass marks: for the beginner level, use these metrics only against the rider's own history (trends).
- Early peak-power and duration gains that rise naturally with training are progress signals — do not apply the power-distortion heuristics from "Power Data Quality and Calibration" (those target unexplained jumps).
- The first indicator of progress is how often, how long and how consistently the rider rides; power and capability metrics come second.

## Prescription and communication

- Keep advice to 2–3 items; each lands on "what to do next time / next week". Explain IF/TSS/CTL in plain words the first time they appear; do not stack abbreviations.
- Acknowledge what is sustainable first (consistency, safety, completion — when the data supports it), then optimize. Never judge with words like "failed / insufficient / poor".
- For the beginner level, downgrade intensity-distribution questions: check whether most riding is at a conversational intensity and whether hard riding is clearly excessive; do not impose polarized/pyramidal pass marks.
- For the beginner level, translate CTL/TSB questions into plain terms: how many rides in the last week or two, how long each, how tired, how recovered. Treat the dossier fitness numbers as trend references only.

## Cadence conventions (all levels; especially relevant for beginners)

- No absolute cadence prescriptions (never "hold 90 rpm"); a naturally low cadence (60–80) at low power is normal, not a problem.
- Cadence rises with relative intensity: 60–75 easy; 80–90 tempo/threshold; 85–100 on climbs and sprints within ability. Higher cadence is needed mainly at high relative intensity, to cap the force of each pedal stroke.
- High cadence is a skill: use it as technique work only (spin-ups: 30 s–1 min up to 100–120 rpm then settle, 4–6 reps); do not ask beginners to hold it for long periods.
- Knee discomfort (anterior knee pain etc.): prefer a smaller gear with higher cadence (per-stroke peak knee load drops by roughly a third at fixed power) and check saddle height first; the real risk scenario is mashing a big gear (cadence below 60 at high relative intensity, common on steep climbs).
- Mechanism (for explaining in reports): at fixed power, cadence only re-allocates between per-stroke peak and repetition count — peak × repetitions is approximately conserved; high cadence lowers the per-stroke peak but not the cumulative load, and at low power the peak is modest anyway.
